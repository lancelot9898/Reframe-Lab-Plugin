# Seller workflow, the step reference

_Built 2026-11-26 from the Reframe Lab seller workflow sheet, as grouped on 2026-11-18. Every row of the sheet is here as a step or a checkbox. Roles are the sheet's. Due rules are relative to a date the deal file records. This is what the deal skills infer against; nothing here is a form to fill in._

Role shorthand: **A** Agent · **TC** Transaction Coordinator · **AS** Assistant · **TL** Team Lead

Dates this workflow keys off: Agreed list price, Appraisal deadline, Appraisal objection deadline, Appraisal result, Approved marketing budget, Buyer inspection objection deadline, Buyer's financing, Closing date, Consultation date, Contract acceptance date, Earnest money deadline, HOA document delivery deadline, HOA document objection deadline, Inspection resolution deadline, Is there an HOA or condo association?, Listing agreement signed on, Loan commitment deadline, Number of offers received, Possession or move-out date, Pre-listing inspection ordered?, Property address, Rent-back end date, Seller disclosure delivery deadline, Seller name(s), Seller's decision, Staging approach, Survey deadline, Target go-live date, Title objection deadline, Title resolution deadline, Was the home built before 1978?


## First conversation

- **Log the new seller and reply within one business day** (AS; due 1 business days after the run starts)
  - [ ] Save the source, contact details, property address and timeline to the client file
  - [ ] Reply by text within 2 hours, or by email by the next business day
  - note: Why it matters: a fast, clear first reply sets the tone for the whole sale.
- **Hold the first call with the seller** (A; due 2 business days after the run starts)
  - note: Cover the basics: why they are selling, their timeline, the home itself, and whether they have worked with another agent on this sale.
- **Send the pre-consultation package** (AS; due 2 business days after the run starts)
  - note: Include the seller guide, an overview of how we work as advisors, and a local market snapshot. Use Reframe Realty branding and current local numbers.
- **Book the listing consultation** (AS; due 3 business days after the run starts; records: Consultation date)
  - note: Confirm the date, time and place, then send a calendar invite with the agenda.
- **Research the property before the consultation** (AS; due 1 days before Consultation date; records: Is there an HOA or condo association?, records: Was the home built before 1978?)
  - note: Pull tax records, past sales, open liens, HOA details and zoning so the consultation starts with facts, not guesses.

## Listing consultation

- **Explain how we work and what we owe the seller** (A; due the day of Consultation date)
  - [ ] Share our experience and what makes our approach different
  - [ ] Explain fiduciary duty: full disclosure, honest pricing guidance, how we negotiate
  - note: Why it matters: the seller should know who works for them, and how.
- **Learn the seller's goals and walk the home** (A; due the day of Consultation date)
  - [ ] Goals, timeline, reasons for selling, rent-back needs, buy first or sell first, prep budget
  - [ ] Walk the home: condition, needed updates, standout features, staging potential
- **Review the listing agreement and the cost of selling** (A; due the day of Consultation date)
  - [ ] Explain the listing agreement: compensation, duration, our duties, cancellation terms
  - [ ] Preview the net sheet: closing costs, title insurance, conveyance taxes, repairs, staging, moving
- **Talk through compensation and the marketing plan** (TL; due the day of Consultation date)
  - note: Be clear about what compensation covers and what the marketing is likely to cost.
- **Set communication preferences** (AS; due the day of Consultation date)
  - note: Agree on the best way to reach the seller, response times, the weekly update day and after-hours contact.

## After the consultation

- **Send the consultation recap and next steps** (AS; due the day of Consultation date)
  - note: Summarize what we discussed, the action items and the timeline. Attach anything we referenced.
- **Deliver the pricing analysis if it wasn't presented at the consultation** (AS; due 2 days after Consultation date)
  - note: A live walkthrough beats an emailed PDF. Book one if it did not happen at the consultation.
- **Follow up on the seller's decision** (AS; due 5 days after Consultation date; records: Seller's decision)
  - note: A respectful check-in to answer any open questions. No pressure. Then save the outcome to the client file.
- **Set 30, 60 and 90 day check-ins** (AS; due 7 days after Consultation date)
  - note: If the seller isn't ready yet, we stay helpful: useful market updates and a check-in at 30, 60 and 90 days.

## Listing agreement

- **Complete the listing agreement with the seller** (A)
  - note: Check every term: price, compensation, duration, and what stays with the home and what goes.
- **Get every owner's signature on the listing agreement** (AS; records: Listing agreement signed on)
  - note: Everyone on the deed signs. If a trust, estate or power of attorney is involved, confirm who has authority to sell. Nothing after this step starts until it is done.
- **Collect the disclosures and the agency acknowledgment** (AS; due 2 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Property condition disclosure and any other state-required forms
  - [ ] Signed agency disclosure, so the seller knows how we represent them
  - note: Connecticut and Colorado use different forms. Use the current state form for this property.
- **Complete the lead-based paint disclosure** (A; due 3 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - note: Federal law requires it for homes built before 1978.
- **Collect the HOA documents and contacts** (AS; due 5 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - note: Fees, special assessments, rules, rental restrictions and the reserve study, plus the management contact.
- **Order the title report and confirm the listing paperwork** (A; due 3 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Order the preliminary title report or title commitment
  - [ ] Listing agreement, fully signed
  - [ ] Agency disclosure acknowledgment
  - [ ] Seller property disclosure
  - [ ] Lead-based paint disclosure, if built before 1978
  - [ ] HOA disclosure or resale certificate, if there is an HOA
  - [ ] Square footage disclosure, if required
  - note: Why it matters: liens, easements and boundary issues are far easier to fix now than mid-deal.

## Setting up the file

- **Open the file, confirm ownership and order any warranty** (TC; due 3 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Create the file in reZEN and upload the agreement, disclosures and early documents
  - [ ] Check the legal description and everyone on title against county records
  - [ ] Set the seller up in the TC client portal (AFrame) for updates, feedback and documents
  - [ ] Order a home warranty if the seller wants one; note the cost and coverage
- **Set the listing timeline** (TC; due 1 days after Listing agreement signed on; records: Target go-live date)
  - note: Map the key dates: photos, staging, MLS entry, open house and first showings.
- **Welcome the seller and line up access** (AS; due 2 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Share the property details, the seller's expectations and any special considerations with the team
  - [ ] Send the welcome packet: timeline, what to expect, key contacts, how we will communicate
  - [ ] Confirm showing instructions: lockbox, pets, alarm, times to avoid, tenant notice
  - [ ] Arrange access for the photographer, stager, inspector and contractors
- **Introduce the TC and team to the seller** (A; due 2 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - note: Explain who handles what and share direct contact details. Who handles what: the TC also sends the seller an intro email.

## Pricing

- **Build the pricing analysis** (A; due 10 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Pull 3 to 5 closed sales from the last 6 months within about half a mile (adjust for the area)
  - [ ] Adjust each comp for size, lot, condition, upgrades, age, location and view
  - [ ] Review market conditions: days on market, inventory, buyer demand, seasonality
  - [ ] Prepare the presentation with visuals and price per square foot
- **Walk the seller through the pricing analysis and strategy** (A; due 7 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Present it live and explain every comp and the method
  - [ ] Discuss pricing to the market versus pricing to negotiate, and what overpricing costs in time
  - [ ] Agree in advance when we will revisit the price if the market is quiet
  - [ ] Check the price against the likely appraisal range and plan for a low appraisal
- **Agree on the list price and prepare the net sheet** (A; due 5 days before Target go-live date; records: Agreed list price, records: Approved marketing budget)
  - note: Write down the agreed price and the reasoning behind it. If the seller's hopes and the data disagree, talk about it openly. Then show estimated proceeds after all costs and payoffs.

## Preparing the home

- **Recommend a pre-listing inspection** (A; due 5 days after Listing agreement signed on; records: Pre-listing inspection ordered?)
  - note: Optional, but it finds issues before a buyer does and makes the sale more predictable.
- **Review the inspection results with the seller** (A; due 12 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - note: Sort findings into safety, structural and cosmetic, and weigh cost against return.
- **Prep the exterior** (A; due 14 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Curb appeal: mow, trim, mulch, seasonal flowers, edge the walkways
  - [ ] Power wash the driveway, walkways, siding, deck and fence
  - [ ] Touch up paint on the front door, trim, shutters and garage door
  - [ ] Clean the gutters and downspouts
  - [ ] Make sure every exterior light works
  - [ ] Clean the windows inside and out
- **Handle repairs, permits and interior updates** (A; due 21 days after Listing agreement signed on)
  - [ ] Verify permits for past and current work with the building department
  - [ ] Get repair bids, manage the timeline and keep every receipt for the disclosure file
  - [ ] Patch walls, fix doors and fixtures, repair damaged flooring
  - [ ] Paint in light, warm neutrals, including ceilings and trim
  - [ ] Update dated light fixtures and brighten every room
- **Coordinate cleaning, decluttering and odor treatment** (AS; due 4 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Professional deep clean: carpets, tile, grout, appliances, bathrooms
  - [ ] Declutter rooms, closets and storage; clear the counters
  - [ ] Treat any pet, smoke or cooking odor at the source
- **Get the home camera-ready and showing-ready** (A; due 4 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Store family photos and personal items so buyers can picture themselves there
  - [ ] Set up smart home features: thermostat, lighting scenes, music, security instructions
  - [ ] Give the seller a showing checklist: lights on, blinds open, pets secured, beds made, counters clear
- **Choose the staging approach** (A; due 10 days after Listing agreement signed on; records: Staging approach)
  - note: Consider the price point, buyer expectations, and whether the home is vacant or lived in.
- **Coordinate professional staging** (A; due 4 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Walk through with the stager and agree on the plan and budget
  - [ ] Finish the installation before photos; every room camera-ready

## Marketing and launch

- **Schedule professional photography** (TC; due 5 days before Target go-live date)
  - note: HDR photos, plus twilight shots when they fit the home. Staging must be finished first. Who handles what: the TC books it once the agent confirms the plan and details.
- **Order the extra media** (A; due 5 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Drone photos, if the lot or views call for it (check local drone rules)
  - [ ] 3D tour for out-of-area buyers
  - [ ] Video walkthrough
  - [ ] Floor plan with room dimensions
  - note: Skip anything that doesn't fit this home.
- **Approve the media and prepare the listing copy and print pieces** (A; due 2 days before Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Approve the photos and media: accurate, well edited, no personal items missed
  - [ ] Write the MLS description: top features, lifestyle, neighborhood, fair housing compliant
  - [ ] Seller confirms every feature and upgrade is accurate
  - [ ] Print-ready feature sheet, brochures and flyers for showings
  - [ ] Single-property website, if it fits the home
  - [ ] Home book at the property: disclosures, survey, HOA documents, utility costs, upgrade list
- **Enter the listing in the MLS and put up the sign** (TC; due the day of Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Every required field checked: square feet, beds, baths, lot, year built, features, disclosures
  - [ ] Photos in order, leading with the best exterior shot
  - [ ] 3D tour, video and floor plan links attached and tested
  - [ ] Showing instructions and lockbox details set
  - [ ] Yard sign and riders installed
  - [ ] Next day: check how it shows on Zillow, Realtor.com and other sites
  - note: Who handles what: the TC enters the listing once the agent sends the photos in order, media links and showing instructions. The agent reviews it before it goes live. The TC orders signs when a sign vendor is set up. The MLS controls what other sites show, so errors get fixed in the MLS.
- **Announce the listing to our network** (AS; due the day of Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Just Listed email to our database, local buyers and agents
  - [ ] Tell the office and our agent network; invite agents to a broker preview
- **Launch the marketing** (A; due 5 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Share on our social channels with the professional photos
  - [ ] Targeted digital ads, if they are in the budget
  - [ ] Post the video to YouTube and social, titled with the address and neighborhood
  - [ ] Just Listed postcards to the 100 to 200 nearest homes

## On the market

- **Run the showing service** (TC; due the day of Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Turn on the showing service with the seller's notification preferences
  - [ ] Handle showing requests around the seller's schedule
  - [ ] Collect feedback after each showing for the weekly update
  - note: The showing software handles most requests and feedback automatically.
- **Review feedback with the seller and follow up with buyer agents** (A; due 7 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Look for patterns in price, condition or layout comments and discuss changes
  - [ ] Call buyer agents within 24 hours of a showing to gauge interest and answer questions
  - note: Repeats every week the home is on the market.
- **Send the weekly seller update** (AS; due 7 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Showing count, feedback summary, market changes and any strategy adjustments
  - [ ] New competing listings, price changes and recent sales worth knowing about
  - note: Goes out on the agreed day every week until the home is under contract.
- **Plan and host the first open house** (A; due 14 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Pick the date: within the first one to two weeks; broker open, public, or both
  - [ ] Promote it: MLS open house field, social posts, directional signs, email
  - [ ] Prepare sign-in sheets, brochures, disclosures, cards and refreshments
  - [ ] Host it: greet visitors, point out features, note serious interest
- **Follow up with visitors and report back to the seller** (AS; due 15 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Contact every visitor within 24 hours with more information or a second showing
  - [ ] Report visitor count, feedback, interested parties and next steps to the seller
- **Review the price and keep the listing fresh** (A; due 21 days after Target go-live date)
  - [ ] Check the price plan: days on market, showing volume and feedback patterns
  - [ ] Refresh photos or marketing when the season or staging changes
  - [ ] Remind the seller about upkeep: lawn, cleanliness, clutter, especially before open houses

## Offers and negotiation

- **Log each offer as it arrives** (A; records: Number of offers received)
  - note: Record when it came in, the buyer's agent and the terms. Track every offer.
- **Analyze every offer and agree on a strategy** (A)
  - [ ] Call the buyer's lender, or confirm proof of funds for cash
  - [ ] Compare price, contingencies, closing date, financing, escalation, concessions and inclusions
  - [ ] Prepare a net sheet for each offer so they compare side by side
  - [ ] Review any request for buyer-agent compensation openly with the seller
  - [ ] Explain the strengths and weaknesses of each, with a recommendation and no pressure
  - [ ] Decide together: counter, accept or decline, and the tone and timeline of the response
- **Run a fair multiple-offer process** (A)
  - note: Ask for highest and best, keep the process transparent, and follow the seller's instructions.
- **Negotiate to final terms in writing** (A)
  - [ ] Prepare and send any counter-offer with every term addressed
  - [ ] Put every agreed change in writing; nothing verbal-only
  - [ ] Review the accepted offer: every signature, initial and date, no blanks, clear deadlines
- **Get every signature on the accepted contract** (A; records: Buyer's financing)
  - [ ] All buyers and sellers have signed
  - [ ] Fully executed contract and every addendum shared with all parties and on file
  - note: The financing answer below shows or hides the appraisal and loan steps.
- **Record the contract dates** (TC; records: Contract acceptance date, records: Earnest money deadline, records: Seller disclosure delivery deadline, records: Buyer inspection objection deadline, records: Inspection resolution deadline, records: Appraisal deadline, records: Appraisal objection deadline, records: HOA document delivery deadline, records: HOA document objection deadline, records: Loan commitment deadline, records: Title objection deadline, records: Title resolution deadline, records: Survey deadline, records: Closing date, records: Possession or move-out date, records: Rent-back end date)
  - note: Enter every deadline from the executed contract. These dates set the due dates for everything that follows, so leave a field blank only if the contract doesn't have that deadline.
- **Share the contract and update the MLS** (TC; due 1 days after Contract acceptance date)
  - [ ] Send the executed contract to the title company or closing attorney and file it in reZEN
  - [ ] Confirm the buyer received earnest money instructions: amount, deadline, where it goes
  - [ ] Change the MLS status to under contract, with any backup offer instructions
  - [ ] Walk the seller through every contract deadline and what happens if one is missed

## Under contract

- **Confirm the earnest money was deposited** (TC; due the day of Earnest money deadline)
  - note: Get the receipt from the escrow holder, file it, and let everyone know.
- **Deliver the seller disclosures to the buyer** (A; due the day of Seller disclosure delivery deadline)
  - note: Send the completed disclosures to the buyer's agent by the contract deadline and keep proof of delivery.
- **Keep the title work moving** (TC; due 7 days after Contract acceptance date)
  - [ ] Follow up on the title search and flag issues early
  - [ ] Send what title needs: survey, HOA documents, payoff statements, trust or court documents
- **Review the title commitment and clear any issues** (A; due the day of Title objection deadline)
  - note: Liens, judgments, easements and boundary questions get resolved with the seller well before closing.
- **Coordinate access for the buyer's inspections** (TC; due 5 days after Contract acceptance date)
  - [ ] Home inspection: confirm the time and that the seller will be out
  - [ ] Specialty inspections as needed: radon, sewer, pest, well, septic, structural
- **Respond to the buyer's inspection requests** (A; due the day of Inspection resolution deadline)
  - [ ] Review the report and requests: safety, structural, cosmetic, unreasonable
  - [ ] Advise the seller on what to repair, credit or decline, with repair estimates
  - [ ] Negotiate a written resolution and document every agreed repair or credit
- **Confirm agreed repairs are done and documented** (A; due 7 days before Closing date)
  - note: Collect receipts, invoices and completion photos and share them with the buyer's agent. Who handles what: the agent coordinates repairs; the TC files the paperwork.
- **Send the appraiser a support package** (A; due 7 days after Contract acceptance date)
  - note: Include the pricing analysis, upgrades with costs, comparable sales and anything unique about the home.
- **Coordinate access for the appraisal** (TC; due 10 days after Contract acceptance date)
  - note: The home should be clean and staged. The agent meets the appraiser where that is allowed.
- **Review the appraisal** (A; due the day of Appraisal deadline; records: Appraisal result)
  - note: Compare the appraised value to the contract price and flag any issues.
- **Negotiate the appraisal gap** (A; due the day of Appraisal objection deadline)
  - note: Options: adjust the price, the buyer covers the gap, meet in the middle, or challenge it with better comps.
- **Deliver the HOA documents and track the buyer's review** (TC; due the day of HOA document delivery deadline)
  - [ ] Send bylaws, financials, minutes, reserve study, rules and pending assessments
  - [ ] Track the buyer's review deadline and follow up as it gets close
- **Track every contingency and get removals in writing** (TC; due 10 days before Closing date)
  - [ ] Watch each deadline: inspection, appraisal, financing, HOA, sale of the buyer's home
  - [ ] File a written removal or waiver for each one in reZEN
- **Check in on the buyer's loan** (TC; due the day of Loan commitment deadline)
  - note: Regular check-ins with the lender; escalate any delay that threatens closing.

## Getting to closing

- **Confirm the closing date, time and place with everyone** (TC; due 7 days before Closing date)
  - note: Title company or closing attorney, seller, buyer, agents and lender. Send calendar invites.
- **Confirm move-out plans and ownership documents** (A; due 7 days before Closing date)
  - [ ] Move-out: clean, belongings out unless there is a rent-back, every included item stays
  - [ ] Trust documents or power of attorney, if they apply
  - [ ] Divorce decree or court order, if it applies
  - [ ] Utility transfer information
- **Confirm the buyer's lender has issued clear to close** (TC; due 4 days before Closing date)
  - note: Follow up with the lender directly and escalate if it is late.
- **Transfer or cancel utilities and services** (AS; due 3 days before Closing date)
  - note: Electric, gas, water, internet, trash, lawn care, security, pool service.
- **Check the settlement statement and schedule the walkthrough** (TC; due 3 days before Closing date)
  - [ ] Check the numbers: price, compensation, credits, prorations, payoffs
  - [ ] Confirm repairs are documented with receipts, photos and warranties
  - [ ] Send title or the closing attorney everything they need: payoff letters, ID, HOA certificates, survey, power of attorney
  - [ ] Schedule the buyer's final walkthrough with the buyer's agent
  - note: Who handles what: the TC gives the statement a once-over; the agent and seller review it in depth.
- **Confirm the paperwork is on file before closing** (TC; due 2 days before Closing date)
  - [ ] Title commitment
  - [ ] Survey, if available
  - [ ] Deed for review
  - [ ] Earnest money receipt
  - [ ] Inspection report, requests and resolution
  - [ ] Appraisal report, if financed
  - [ ] HOA package for the buyer, if there is an HOA
  - [ ] Repair receipts and documentation
  - [ ] Contingency removals and waivers
  - [ ] Settlement statement
  - [ ] Compensation disbursement authorization
  - [ ] Home warranty documents, if ordered
  - [ ] Final walkthrough confirmation
- **Walk the seller through the settlement statement** (A; due 2 days before Closing date)
  - note: Every charge and credit, compared against the net sheet, with time for questions.
- **Schedule the staging pickup** (AS; due 2 days before Closing date)
- **Prepare the home and keys, and resolve walkthrough issues** (A; due 1 days before Closing date)
  - [ ] Broom-clean, every agreed item in place, no move-out damage
  - [ ] Gather keys, mailbox key, garage remotes, alarm codes, manuals and warranties
  - [ ] Fix missing items, damage or unfinished repairs before closing whenever possible

## Closing day

- **Be at the closing and hand over the keys** (A; due the day of Closing date)
  - [ ] Attend in person or coordinate remote signing
  - [ ] Review the deed, settlement statement and tax forms with the seller
  - [ ] Deliver keys, remotes and access items as the contract says
- **Confirm signatures, recording and disbursement** (TC; due the day of Closing date)
  - [ ] Every document signed; the closing package is complete
  - [ ] The deed is recorded and ownership has transferred
  - [ ] Compensation paid out as the agreement says

## After closing

- **Share moving resources and close out the listing** (AS; due 1 days after Closing date)
  - [ ] Send the moving checklist, change of address steps and utility setup guide
  - [ ] Mark the MLS listing sold with the final price, date and concessions
  - [ ] Remove the lockbox, sign and marketing materials; cancel the showing service
- **Finish the transaction file** (TC; due 3 days after Closing date)
  - [ ] Audit the file: every document uploaded and compliant
  - [ ] Update the client record with the sale details
  - [ ] Recorded deed confirmation
  - [ ] Final closing package
  - [ ] Compensation statement
- **Send a closing gift and handwritten thank-you** (A; due 7 days after Closing date)
  - note: Personal and specific to this seller. Nothing generic.
- **Ask how we did** (AS; due 14 days after Closing date)
  - [ ] Send the short satisfaction survey
  - [ ] Invite a review on Google or Zillow with direct links
- **Stay in their corner after the move** (AS; due 28 days after Closing date)
  - [ ] Ask whether anyone they know could use our help
  - [ ] Set up home value updates and seasonal check-ins
  - [ ] Schedule a home anniversary note every year
