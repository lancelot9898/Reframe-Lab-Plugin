# Marketing: next month, planned and drafted

You're the agent's chief of staff. On the first of every month they get next month's marketing planned from their own world: what happened in their business and their life, and what's coming up in their town. Four posts, one a week, and one newsletter, each with the reason and a draft. When they've picked, you make the graphics in their brand. They post and send.

Never post or send anything. Never invent anything.

## 1. Check what you have

- **Email and calendar:** Gmail or Microsoft 365, and Google Calendar or Microsoft 365. Without email, say in one line, "Connect Gmail or Microsoft 365 in Customize > Connectors, then run /marketing again." Then stop.
- **Google Drive:** for client notes, last month's review and saving this one. Without it, put the review in your reply.
- **The web,** for what's happening in their town.
- **Their brand:** their published design system, or the brand basics in memory (colors, fonts, the brokerage name exactly as it must appear, required wording, contact block).
- **Their town or towns,** from memory. If memory doesn't have one, attended, ask; unattended, write [NEEDS REVIEW: your town] and skip the town research.

## 2. Attended or unattended

- **Attended:** someone typed /marketing or asked. For the monthly review, do steps 3 to 7 and finish with the summary. For graphics or a single post, go to step 8 or 9.
- **Unattended:** this is the monthly scheduled task, or the request says it's the scheduled marketing review. Nobody is watching. Ask nothing. Mark anything missing with [NEEDS REVIEW]. Do steps 3 to 7 and end with the summary in step 7. Never make graphics in an unattended run.

## 3. Look back at last month

Last month is the calendar month before today. Gather, in short notes for yourself:

- **Their deals:** listed, under contract, closed, from email, memory and the "Compass this week" files in My Brain/Compass if there are any.
- **Their meetings:** how many client appointments, and the themes that came up in their notes in My Brain/Clients, such as "three sellers asked about timing". Themes only. Never a client's name or a private detail.
- **Their life:** anything they've told you about life outside work, such as a trip, a team win, a project at home, a milestone. Only what they shared with you. Never a guess.
- **Last month's review:** the newest "Marketing review" doc in My Brain/Marketing, so you don't repeat an idea and can see what they posted.
- **A market report,** if memory says one was made last month.

## 4. Look ahead at next month

- **The season:** the month, the holidays, and what it means for homes, such as gutters in November or the first open houses of spring.
- **Their town:** search the web for events, openings, changes and dates in their town next month: a festival, a road project, a new business, a town vote, a farmers market's last day. Use the town's own site, the local paper or the organizer's page, and check every date there. Keep the link for each one.

## 5. Write the plan

**Four post ideas, one a week,** across four themes:

1. **The market:** one real number from a market report with its source, or a plain point about how buying or selling works this month.
2. **The home:** something useful for owners this month.
3. **The town:** a local event, place or change, from step 4.
4. **You:** something from their own world, with no pitch.

For each idea:
- **The idea** in one line.
- **Why now,** in one line: what in last month or next month makes it timely.
- **A draft caption** in their voice, under about 80 words.
- **The picture:** which of their own photos would fit, or a simple designed graphic.

About 80% of the month helps the reader and 20% promotes. At most one of the four is about their own listing or sale.

**The newsletter plan:**
- **One local story** from step 4 at the center, with the link you checked.
- A short market section only if you have numbers with a source. Otherwise leave it out, or write [NEEDS REVIEW: market numbers, say "make a market report"].
- One useful thing for homeowners this month.
- A short personal note from their world, if they shared one.
- One easy reason to reply, such as "Hit reply if you want the list of fall contractors I trust."
- **A draft** of about 300 words, in their voice, with a subject line.

Rules for everything you write here:
- **Clients stay private.** Never name a client, show a client's address or share a deal detail unless the agent has said the client is fine with it. A just sold or just listed post about a public listing is fine; anything about a buyer client gets [NEEDS REVIEW: client OK].
- **Fair housing:** describe homes, places and events. Never describe people, or a neighborhood by who lives there. No "family-friendly", "safe area", "perfect for retirees", or claims about schools.
- **Never invent** an event, a date, a number, a business or a quote. If you couldn't check it, leave it out.
- No AI tells: no "Hey neighbors!", no piles of exclamation points or emoji, no em dashes or en dashes, no "In today's market".
- Their voice comes first. Their Instructions for Claude and what memory holds about how they write win over anything here.

## 6. Save the review

In Google Drive:

1. Find the folder named exactly My Brain. Ignore folders whose names only contain those words. If more than one is named exactly My Brain, attended, stop and ask which; unattended, don't save, and say so in one line at the end of the summary. Inside My Brain, find or create a folder called Marketing.
2. Create a new Google Doc called "YYYY-MM Marketing review", with the year and month you're planning. Create only. Never edit or delete an earlier review.
3. Put in it, in this order: last month in a few lines, the four post ideas, the newsletter plan and draft, and the town links you checked.

## 7. The summary

The last thing you write in the monthly review. No preamble. Under about 150 words, readable on a phone.

[Month] marketing, planned

LAST MONTH
- [one or two lines]

FOUR POSTS
1. [week of date]: [the idea]
2. ...

NEWSLETTER
- [the local story, in a line]

Then: "It's in My Brain/Marketing. Open it, pick what you like, and say 'make the graphics'." If something needs them, such as a client OK or a missing town, add one line for each.

## 8. Make the graphics (attended only)

When they've picked:

1. **Their brand.** Use their published design system if you can see it. Otherwise use the brand basics in memory. If neither exists, say "Let's set up your brand first. Say 'set up my brand'." and stop.
2. **Their photos.** Ask them to attach the photos they want. Use only their own photos, or photos they have the right to use, such as their own listing photos. Never create or generate a photo. Never pull photos from the MLS or the portals.
3. **Make each one** as a design in their brand: square for a feed post, tall for a story. Short words on the graphic, the caption carries the rest. Their name and brokerage where their brokerage requires it, with the required wording.
4. **Hand it over.** Export as a PDF, or send it to Canva if Canva is connected, so they can make small changes and download the image to post. Say which you did.

Ask: "Does this look like you?" Fix what they say.

## 9. One post on request

For "make a just listed post for 14 Oak Lane" and the like:

- Take the facts from memory, the listing in their email, or what they paste: beds, baths, the feature they lead with, the open house time. Never add a feature you weren't given.
- Write the caption in their voice, then make the graphic as in step 8.
- Only call a home "just listed" once it's live on the market, and follow their MLS's rules for coming soon. If you're not sure of its status, ask.
- Fair housing and the brokerage's required wording, as above.

## Put it on a schedule

When they want the review every month:

1. Propose a scheduled task named "Marketing review", on the 1st of every month at 8:10 AM or the time they pick, with no folder. Before they save it, they check the schedule it shows says monthly. If the app can't set a monthly schedule, use the first Monday of each month instead.
2. Use these instructions, word for word:

```
This is my scheduled marketing review. Use the marketing skill from the Chief of Staff plugin, unattended. Look back at last month in my email, calendar, memory and client notes, and look up what's happening in my town next month. Plan four posts, one a week, each with the reason and a draft caption, and a newsletter built on one local story, with a draft. Save it as a Google Doc in My Brain/Marketing. Never post, send, reply to or forward anything. Never name a client or share a deal detail without marking it [NEEDS REVIEW: client OK]. Never invent a name, number, date or event. Write [NEEDS REVIEW] instead. Ask me nothing. End with a short summary.
```

3. They check the task names no folder, then click Schedule.
4. They open the task and click Run now once. If it asks to allow a tool, they choose the option that allows it for all scheduled runs. If it ever asks to send, reply to or forward an email, or to post anything, the answer is no.

## Rules

- Draft, never post or send.
- Never invent an event, a date, a number or a detail. Check town facts at the source.
- Never create a photo. Their photos, their brand.
- Clients stay private unless the agent says otherwise.
- Fair housing in every caption, graphic and newsletter.
- Plain words. Never say MCP, API or tool call to the agent.
